Research question

Given a finished physical object, which predecessor states remain possible after geometry, gravity, topology, access, materials, archaeology and evidence are applied as constraints?

P(history | terminal state, evidence, laws)

This is a working research program, not a claim that the true construction sequence has already been recovered.

From monument to inverse ontology

The model separates the present monument from the original as-built state, matter from intentional voids, geometric height from historical time, and inferential backward reconstruction from literal time reversal.

Positive matter

Blocks, mortar, casing, granite, bedrock and their interfaces become a material graph with mass, density, strength, provenance and contact relations.

Negative topological objects

Chambers, corridors and shafts are represented as deliberately preserved exclusions of matter with their own birth, closure, connectivity and disappearance.

Chrono-causal field

Each material or void element receives an inferred time window and dependency lineage instead of assuming that altitude is a proxy for date.

X(T) → predecessor states → dependency DAG → material/void birth fields → candidate construction histories → counterfactual predictions

Core equations

The current prototype separates four inverse coordinates instead of pretending a single slider is historical time.

Inverse time

τ = T − t

The inferential coordinate moves backward; gravitational acceleration does not reverse sign.

Removed mass fraction

ΓM = (1 − u)³

For an ideal pyramid dismantled from its apex, u is the fraction of full height remaining.

Released potential energy

ΓG = 1 − 6u² + 8u³ − 3u⁴

Mass, height and gravitational potential are nonlinearly different clocks.

Counterfactual void mass

mC⁻ = −∫VC ρref(x) dV

Signed reference bookkeeping only — not physical negative mass.

Inverse-removable front

A⁻(X) = {e : maximal(e) ∧ Stable(X−e) ∧ Accessible(e)}

Topological/causal order must be combined with structural and access constraints.

Counterfactual test

ŶH = C(H) ; compare ŶH with Yobserved

A credible construction hypothesis must predict traces we can seek in the monument.
